Answers from SEC filings that show their page.

A question-answering system over 360 real 10-K, 10-Q and 8-K filings. Every answer cites the filing and page it came from, or says the information isn’t there. Each part of it was kept only if a measured experiment on the FinanceBench benchmark showed it helped.

On 100 benchmark questions it never saw during tuning, it answers 49% correctly. Plain vector search manages 15%.

How it does on questions it never saw

FinanceBench’s 150 questions were split once: 50 for building and tuning, 100 held back. The held-back set was run a single time, after every decision was made.

The improvement is +34 points, with a 95% confidence interval of +24 to +44 (paired bootstrap over the same questions). It also declined all 30 questions in a separate set built to be unanswerable, such as companies outside the corpus and years with no filing yet.

    What was tried, and what was kept

    Each experiment changed one thing and was compared with the previous best on the same 50 tuning questions. Changes that didn’t clearly help were dropped, including some that sound like best practice.

    How an answer is made

    1. Read the question

      Rules pick out the company and fiscal year. They’re right on 50 of 50 tuning questions for the company and 48 of 50 for the year.

    2. Search the right filings

      Vector search over 81,517 passages, restricted to that company and year, returns the 50 closest passages. If nothing matches, the filter loosens.

    3. Rerank

      A cross-encoder (bge-reranker-v2-m3) reads each passage against the question and keeps the best 5.

    4. Answer with citations

      A local model (Qwen3 14B via Ollama) answers only from those passages and cites them. When they don’t contain the answer, it says so instead of guessing.

    5. Check it

      Every change is scored on 50 tuning questions. A gate on every push to GitHub re-checks retrieval quality, and fails the build if it drops.

    Where it falls short

    • The model is now the weak link. Even when handed the exact right pages, it answers 54% of the tuning questions correctly, and 18 of its 51 held-out misses happened with the right evidence in front of it.
    • It’s cautious. It declines 26% of answerable questions, sometimes with the evidence already retrieved.
    • It grades itself. The judge is the same local model as the answerer. The judge passed its sanity checks (98% or better on correctness), but a person hasn’t yet graded its grades.
    • Reranking costs time: about 6–7 seconds per question on a laptop.
