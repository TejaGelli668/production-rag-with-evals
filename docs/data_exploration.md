## Implications for the pipeline

_Filled in by hand below, from the numbers above._

1. **Page numbering is settled.** `evidence_page_num` equals the PyMuPDF page index (all 189 items).
   Chunks store the 0-based PyMuPDF `page_num`, and retrieval metrics compare it to the label directly.
   No offset handling is needed.
2. **No OCR needed.** No document lacks a text layer, and evidence pages always have text. The ~2%
   low-text pages are covers and separators. So PyMuPDF is a workable baseline, and parsing work in E3
   can focus on **table structure**, not OCR.
3. **Scale calls for resumable, cached ingestion.** `full` is ~53k pages / ~45M tokens, roughly
   90–100k chunks at 512 tokens. Local embedding is feasible but slow, so ingestion must batch, cache,
   and resume. Develop on `focused` (12k pages / 10M tokens) first.
4. **Hard negatives come from the same company.** Each question's company has a median of 9 filings in
   the corpus (max 14): other years and other filing types. Retrieval has to tell 3M FY2018 from
   3M FY2019, so extracting company and fiscal year as filters (E5) should be the biggest retrieval
   win in `full` mode.
5. **23% of questions need more than one page.** Report Page Hit@k ("any gold page in top-k") and
   Page Recall@k ("fraction of gold pages in top-k") separately; final k cannot be 1.
6. **Only 35% of answers are bare numbers.** Correctness scoring needs two stages: a numeric match
   with unit/rounding tolerance where the gold answer is numeric, and an LLM judge for sentence answers
   (which are often a number plus a judgment, e.g. "No. The quick ratio was 0.96…").
7. **Weak text matches on a few table-heavy pages** (Corning 2020/2021, CVS 2018: similarity 0.39–0.49,
   though still best at offset 0) suggest PyMuPDF's table text differs from upstream's extraction.
   Use these as spot checks when comparing parsers in E3.
8. **Breakdowns by reasoning type cover only 100 questions.** The 50 `metrics-generated` questions
   have no reasoning label.
